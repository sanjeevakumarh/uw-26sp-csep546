- A6.a
	- 1. **Percentage of kids in two-parent households**: Variability driven by welfare policies (e.g., pre-1996 AFDC eligibility rules penalizing married households), family law, social safety net programs, and education policy — differences in education access, requirements, and cost of education across states affect economic stability which in turn influences family structure.
	- 2. **Percentage of police that are white**: Shaped by hiring policies, affirmative action mandates, and civil service exam requirements set by local government.
	- 3. **Number of homeless people counted on the street**: Influenced by housing policy, zoning laws that affect cost of housing, financial support and lending policies (e.g., redlining, mortgage access), mental health funding, and shelter investment decisions by municipalities.
-
- A6.b
	- 1. **Number of homeless on street**: High crime areas may cause displacement and reduce property values, increasing homelessness — rather than homelessness causing crime.
	- 2. **Police per population**: Communities with high crime rates hire more police in response — the large police presence is a *result* of crime, not a cause.
	- 3. **Percent of vacant/boarded housing**: Crime drives residents out and depresses investment, leading to vacant properties — the vacancy is an effect of crime, not a driver.
